## 5. The Nine Pillars of Language

The nine pillars form a complete, ordered spectrum. Implementations MAY emphasize subsets for particular modalities, but a full-domain claim requires all nine.

### 5.1 STRUCTURAL (The Skeleton)

The abstract combinatorial architecture of language: constituency, dependency, hierarchical embedding, and the generative rules that permit infinite expression from finite means.

**Normative requirements**
- MUST expose a machine-readable structural representation (tree, graph, or equivalent) for every well-formed utterance or text.
- MUST support reversible transformations between surface and structural forms without loss of identity-relevant content.
- SHALL remain compatible with both formal-linguistic and distributional (embedding) models.

### 5.2 PHONOLOGICAL (The Flesh)

The material, acoustic–articulatory substrate of spoken (and signed) language: phonemes, prosody, tone, rhythm, and the physical signal that carries structure.

**Normative requirements**
- MUST treat phonological form as a first-class, reversible layer.
- MUST interoperate cleanly with VASP (Sound domain) when the medium is audio.
- SHOULD preserve prosodic and affective contours under xessability transforms.

### 5.3 MORPHOLOGICAL (The Skin)

The surface form layer: inflection, derivation, compounding, and the visible/audible shape that interfaces with the world.

**Normative requirements**
- MUST distinguish morphological operations from pure phonological or semantic ones.
- MUST support morphological generation and analysis for all claimed languages or language families.
- SHALL treat orthographic and morphological variation as accessibility parameters, not as semantic corruption.

### 5.4 SEMANTIC (The Voice)

The core meaning-bearing layer: truth-conditional content, reference, predication, and the stable conceptual structure that SCX ultimately receives.

**Normative requirements**
- MUST maintain a clear separation between semantic content and surface form.
- MUST preserve semantic invariants under all permitted xessability transforms.
- SHALL expose uncertainty estimates when semantic interpretation is under-determined.

### 5.5 AFFECTIVE (The Heart)

The emotional, evaluative, and attitudinal force carried by language: valence, arousal, stance, and the felt quality of expression.

**Normative requirements**
- MUST treat affective content as a first-class, parallel channel to pure semantics.
- MUST NOT allow affective modulation to override SAGES ethical invariants (especially Non-Maleficence and Reciprocity).
- SHOULD support explicit affective annotation and reversible affective transforms.

### 5.6 PRAGMATIC (The Scene)

Context, speech acts, implicature, deixis, and the situational embedding that turns semantic content into action and social force.

**Normative requirements**
- MUST model speaker–hearer relations, common ground, and illocutionary force.
- MUST remain sensitive to identity context supplied by ICX.
- SHALL support pragmatic repair and clarification protocols that increase, rather than decrease, mutual understanding.

### 5.7 TYPOGRAPHIC (The Eye)

The visual–spatial realization of language in written and graphical form: layout, type, spacing, color, and the eye’s path through text.

**Normative requirements**
- MUST treat typography as an accessibility and aesthetic layer, not as pure decoration.
- MUST support reversible conversion between typographic and phonological/structural forms.
- SHOULD honor established xessability conventions (contrast, size, spacing, dyslexia-friendly options) by default.

### 5.8 KINETIC (The Body)

The embodied, spatial–temporal performance of language. This pillar explicitly includes:

- Signed languages (ASL and others) as full linguistic systems, not as “translations of speech.”
- Co-speech gesture, postural deixis, and multimodal kinetic channels.
- The proprioceptive and interoceptive traces that accompany linguistic production and comprehension.

**Normative requirements**
- MUST treat signed languages as native Language systems with equal status to spoken and written modalities.
- MUST expose kinematic representations (joint trajectories, handshape sequences, spatial loci) for signed and gestural content.
- SHALL integrate with Vessel Binding Protocol and Somatic Continuity Standard for body–identity continuity.
- Haptic and Braille renderings are treated as cross-modal SUXS-IFO operators, not as a separate pillar at this time.

### 5.9 GENEALOGICAL (The Roots)

The historical, cultural, and provenance lineage of linguistic forms: etymology, borrowing, dialect continua, and the verifiable chain of transmission.

**Normative requirements**
- MUST maintain or reconstruct genealogical provenance for significant expressions when claimed.
- MUST support reversible tracing from contemporary form back to attested or reconstructed ancestors.
- SHALL treat language change as a legitimate evolutionary process that nevertheless remains bound by identity and ethical continuity constraints.

## 6. Normative Requirements (System-Level)

A conformant Language implementation **MUST**:

1. Realize all nine pillars or explicitly declare which subset is supported and why the claim is partial.
2. Preserve the directed mapping SIX → SCX (sensorimotor experience into systemic meaning).
3. Apply accessibility weighting $\omega$ under SUXS-IFO and never silently reduce it.
4. Maintain provenance chains (Genealogical pillar + AuraFS / GuardHash).
5. Respect the reserved vocabulary of the Language and VASP domains (Skeleton, Flesh, Skin, Voice, Heart, Scene, Eye, Body, Roots).
6. Treat ASL and other signed languages as full peer systems under the Kinetic pillar.
7. Enforce relevant SAGES invariants (Identity Continuity, Non-Maleficence, Reciprocity, Ethical Grounding) at every transformation boundary.

A conformant implementation **SHALL NOT**:

- Collapse surface form into semantics in a non-reversible way.
- Override identity-bearing constraints supplied by ICX.
- Treat any modality (spoken, written, signed, multimodal) as ontologically secondary without explicit, reversible justification.

## 7. Compliance Tests

A system is Language-compliant when:

- Every well-formed expression can be projected onto the nine-pillar structure.
- Structural ↔ Surface transforms are reversible within declared bounds.
- Affective and pragmatic channels remain distinguishable from pure semantic content.
- Kinetic representations of signed language are first-class and non-lossy relative to the linguistic content they carry.
- Provenance of significant expressions is recoverable.
- Accessibility weight $\omega$ is never decreased without explicit consent and logging.
- SAGES invariants remain intact after any Language-domain operation.
