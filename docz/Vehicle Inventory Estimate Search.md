# **U.S. Vehicle Inventory Analysis: Early April 2025**

## I. Executive Summary

The United States automotive market experienced a significant tightening of both new and used vehicle inventories at the outset of April 2025. Compared to the previous month and year-ago levels, available supply decreased notably, primarily driven by an anomalous surge in vehicle sales during the final days of March. This sales spike was largely attributed to consumer reaction to the announcement of impending 25% import tariffs.

As of early April 2025, key inventory metrics reflected this rapid shift. New vehicle inventory stood at approximately 2.69 million units, translating to a 70-day supply according to Cox Automotive's methodology. This marked a precipitous drop from 91 days' supply just one month prior. Concurrently, used vehicle inventory was estimated at 2.14 million units, equating to a 39-day supply, down from 42 days at the beginning of March and representing a five-day decrease year-over-year.

The dominant factor influencing this abrupt inventory reduction was the announcement and subsequent implementation of tariffs on imported vehicles, which motivated many consumers to accelerate purchase decisions, pulling demand forward into late March. While this created a temporary surge, persistent challenges, including vehicle affordability constraints and the fact that overall new vehicle inventory remains below pre-pandemic (2019) levels, continue to shape the market landscape. The market outlook is characterized by considerable uncertainty, with industry analysts revising sales forecasts downward in anticipation of potential price increases and a subsequent slowdown in sales momentum once the readily available pre-tariff inventory is depleted.

## II. New Vehicle Inventory Analysis (Early April 2025)

### A. Current Inventory Levels (Total Units)

At the beginning of April 2025, the total count of new vehicles available on dealer lots across the United States was estimated at **2.69 million units**, according to data primarily sourced from Cox Automotive's vAuto Live Market View. This figure represents a substantial **10.2% decline** from the 2.99 million units recorded at the start of March 2025. Furthermore, the early April inventory level was **2.4% lower** than the corresponding period in 2024.

Slight variations in reported figures exist among different analysts during this volatile period. A Cox Automotive report dated April 1st cited a marginally lower figure of 2.67 million units, while S&P Global Mobility estimated the US light-vehicle inventory to be around **2.8 million units** at the close of March. These minor discrepancies likely arise from subtle differences in data collection timing (e.g., "start of April" versus "end of March"), the precise scope of vehicles included (e.g., S&P's focus on "light vehicles"), or proprietary methodologies employed by each firm. However, the overarching narrative remains consistent across sources: a significant and rapid depletion of new vehicle stock occurred during the latter part of March, bringing inventory levels down to the vicinity of 2.7 to 2.8 million units. The critical takeaway is the shared observation of a sharp downward trend from the nearly 3 million units available at the beginning of March.

### B. Days' Supply Assessment

The most widely cited metric for new vehicle days' supply at the start of April 2025, provided by Cox Automotive, was **70 days**. This calculation is based on the estimated daily retail sales rate observed over the *most recent 30-day period* ending in early April.

This 70-day figure marks a dramatic **21-day reduction** from the upwardly revised 91-day supply reported at the beginning of March. Compared to early April 2024, the days' supply was down by 10 days. This contraction effectively returned inventory levels, relative to the sales pace, back to conditions last observed in 2023. It also reversed the trend seen earlier in the quarter, where days' supply had climbed, reaching 89 days at the start of March.

Notably, S&P Global Mobility reported a significantly lower industry-wide estimate of only **43 days' supply** at the end of March. The substantial difference between Cox Automotive's 70 days and S&P Global Mobility's 43 days likely originates from variations in calculation methodologies. Cox Automotive explicitly utilizes a 30-day *retail* sales rate for its denominator. The methodology behind S&P's figure is not detailed in the available materials, but it could potentially employ a shorter, more recent sales period (e.g., the final 7 or 14 days of March). Using a sales rate heavily weighted towards the end-of-March surge would result in a much higher calculated daily sales figure, thereby drastically reducing the days' supply number compared to a 30-day average. Alternatively, differences in the scope of inventory or sales channels included (e.g., fleet vs. retail, specific vehicle segments) could contribute.

Despite the numerical difference, both figures clearly indicate a market that tightened considerably compared to the previous month. The rapid increase in sales velocity during the last week of March is the undisputed cause. While the 70-day figure from Cox Automotive suggests a less severe immediate shortage than the 43-day figure from S&P Global Mobility, both metrics point unequivocally to a sharp reduction in available inventory relative to the heightened sales pace.

### C. Recent Trends (Month-over-Month & Year-over-Year)

The data for early April 2025 reveals a clear and abrupt reversal of recent inventory trends. Both the total number of new vehicles and the calculated days' supply decreased significantly between the beginning of March and the beginning of April 2025. Furthermore, these levels were also lower than those recorded in early April 2024.

This marks a distinct shift from the pattern observed earlier in 2024 and late 2023, where inventory levels had been steadily increasing. The primary catalyst for this sudden downturn was the extraordinary surge in new vehicle sales concentrated in the final days of March. This rush was directly linked to the announcement of impending import tariffs, prompting consumers to finalize purchases before potential price increases took effect. Cox Automotive noted that 30-day average sales rates were running 12% higher year-over-year in early April, reflecting the impact of this late-March activity.

### D. Historical Perspective (Pre-Pandemic Comparison)

To understand the current inventory situation in a broader context, comparison to pre-pandemic levels is essential. Analysis from Cox Automotive indicates that despite the recovery seen through 2024, the new vehicle market had not fully returned to historical norms even *before* the recent tariff-induced dip.

As of early April 2025, total new vehicle inventory remained significantly constrained compared to 2019 levels, estimated to be **31% lower**. Correspondingly, the days' supply (using the approximately 71.5-day figure associated with the 2019 comparison data from Cox) was **24% below** the 2019 benchmark.

This persistent deficit relative to pre-pandemic conditions suggests that factors beyond immediate disruptions are influencing inventory levels. While the acute manufacturing shutdowns and semiconductor shortages of the pandemic era have eased, the market's return to 2019-level availability has been hindered. This could reflect ongoing, albeit less severe, supply chain bottlenecks, strategic decisions by automakers to maintain leaner inventories and support pricing power, or shifts in production priorities and vehicle mix. The recent tariff announcement and its impact further exacerbate this underlying condition, pushing inventory levels further away from historical averages.

## III. Used Vehicle Inventory Analysis (Early April 2025)

### A. Current Inventory Levels (Total Units)

The total supply of used vehicles available for sale on both franchised and independent dealer lots across the U.S. stood at an estimated **2.14 million units** at the beginning of April 2025. This figure represents a decrease from the 2.18 million units reported at the start of March 2025. Compared to early April 2024, the used vehicle inventory was down by **1%**. Inventory levels had been slightly higher earlier in the year, recorded at 2.23 million units in early February and 2.22 million units in early January.

### B. Days' Supply Assessment

Reflecting the relationship between inventory and sales pace, the days' supply for used vehicles was calculated at **39 days** at the start of April. This metric indicates how long the current inventory would last at the prevailing retail sales rate.

The 39-day supply represents a decrease of four days from the upwardly revised 42-day level at the beginning of March. It was also five days lower than the level observed in April 2024. Earlier in the year, days' supply had been higher, standing at 48 days in both early January and early February. This 39-day figure marks the tightest used vehicle supply for the beginning of April since 2021.

An alternative, related metric is the "Used Retail Days-to-Turn," reported by Black Book via CarEdge, which stood at **43 days** in April 2025. This figure represents the average number of days a used vehicle typically sat on a dealer's lot before being sold. While Days' Supply is a forward-looking measure based on the current sales rate and inventory, Days-to-Turn is a backward-looking average based on actual sales durations. Both metrics, however, point towards a relatively tight used vehicle market in early April. The low 39-day supply suggests that demand, relative to the recent sales pace, was outpacing the available stock. The moderate 43-day turn time indicates that vehicles were, on average, selling reasonably quickly. Both figures being below typical historical norms signal a supply-constrained environment.

### C. Recent Trends (Month-over-Month & Year-over-Year)

Similar to the new vehicle market, used vehicle inventory metrics showed a decline from March to April 2025. Both total units and days' supply decreased month-over-month and were also lower compared to April 2024 levels.

The primary driver for this tightening was a robust used vehicle sales pace during March. Retail sales of used vehicles reached **1.66 million units** in March, marking a significant 9.4% increase compared to February and a 12.2% increase year-over-year. This performance made March 2025 the strongest month for used retail sales since 2021. The typical "Spring Bounce" in used car sales, often fueled by consumers receiving tax refunds, appeared to be a contributing factor to this strong demand. The average tax refund was reported to be up 4% year-over-year as of late March.

### D. Historical Perspective (Comparison to Past Years)

The used vehicle market in early April 2025 continued to operate under supply constraints when viewed historically. The 39-day supply was noted as the tightest for this specific time of year since the highly disrupted market of 2021. Furthermore, it remained nearly two days below the levels seen in pre-pandemic 2019.

Overall used inventory levels were described as tight compared to historical averages, falling below the typical levels observed over the past five years, with the exception of the extreme shortage conditions of 2021. This persistent tightness is largely a legacy effect of the reduced new vehicle production volumes experienced between 2020 and 2022 due to pandemic-related disruptions. Fewer new vehicles sold and leased during that period resulted in a diminished flow of 2-to-4-year-old vehicles entering the used market in subsequent years. The significant drop in the number of vehicles coming off lease in 2025 compared to pre-pandemic norms is a major factor. This fundamental structural limitation on the supply of desirable, younger used vehicles prevents the market from fully returning to pre-pandemic inventory levels, even as demand fluctuates.

### B. Used Vehicles: By Price Tiers and Vehicle Type

Analysis of the used vehicle market reveals significant segmentation, particularly concerning price and vehicle type.

**Price Tiers:** The most striking feature is the extreme inventory tightness at the lower end of the market. Used vehicles priced **below \$15,000** had only **28 days' supply** at the start of April. This represents the lowest level of availability for this segment since 2021, down seven days year-over-year and sitting 11 days below the overall used vehicle market average of 39 days. This scarcity highlights the intense pressure on the most affordable segment of the market, driven by ongoing consumer affordability challenges and potentially amplified by faster appreciation observed in older used models as buyers seek lower price points.

**Vehicle Type Trends:** While specific days' supply figures by vehicle type for used retail inventory were not detailed in the provided materials, wholesale market pricing trends offer valuable insights into relative demand and supply dynamics across segments in early April:

- **SUVs and Luxury:** These segments showed the strongest year-over-year wholesale price increases in the first half of April, with SUVs up 5.1% and luxury vehicles up 4.7%. This suggests robust demand and relatively tight supply for these popular categories in the used market. Month-over-month wholesale price increases were also solid for SUVs (+2.0%). Used truck and SUV wholesale prices were generally climbing in April.

- **Trucks and Mid-Size Cars:** These segments saw more moderate year-over-year wholesale price gains (+2.3% for trucks, +0.9% for mid-size cars). Month-over-month increases were +1.9% for trucks and a stronger +2.3% for mid-size cars. Used sedan prices were reported to be rising in April's wholesale market.

- **Compact Cars:** This segment was the only one to show a year-over-year decline in wholesale prices (-0.8%) in early April. The month-over-month increase was also the smallest (+1.5%). This could indicate relatively weaker demand or comparatively better supply for used compact cars versus other segments.

This data suggests a bifurcation within the used market. The sub-\$15,000 segment faces critical supply shortages and likely strong upward price pressure. In the broader market, demand appears particularly strong for used SUVs and luxury vehicles, supporting their values, while the dynamics for trucks and cars show more variation. Relying solely on the average used vehicle listing price (around \$25,180 in early April) obscures these crucial segment-level differences.

### C. Electric Vehicle (EV) Inventory Dynamics

The electric vehicle segment continued its growth trajectory in the first quarter of 2025, although the landscape showed increasing complexity.

- **Sales and Market Share:** Nearly 300,000 new EVs were sold in the U.S. during Q1 2025, an increase of 11.4% compared to Q1 2024. EVs constituted 7.5% of total new vehicle sales in the quarter, up from 7.0% a year prior but down sequentially from 8.7% in Q4 2024. March's BEV share was estimated at 8.5%.

- **Competitive Shifts:** The growth was not uniform across brands. General Motors nearly doubled its EV sales year-over-year in Q1, surpassing both Ford and Hyundai Group in quarterly EV volume. Honda and Acura also contributed significant volume through their partnership with GM. In contrast, market leader Tesla experienced a sales decline of nearly 9% year-over-year in Q1, indicating intensifying competition and potential challenges from its current product cycle ahead of expected refreshes.

- **Pricing:** Average transaction prices for new EVs have decreased substantially over the past year (down 10.8% year-over-year as of January 2024 data), although a slight month-over-month increase was noted in January.

- **Inventory Data:** Specific data on EV inventory levels or days' supply for early April 2025 was not available in the analyzed reports. However, an indicator of increasing activity is that the share of EVs within the Manheim Used Vehicle Value Index (MUVVI) wholesale transaction data reached a record high of over 3% in mid-April. This suggests a growing volume of used EVs being processed through wholesale channels.

The EV market is clearly in a dynamic phase of maturation. While overall adoption is increasing, market share is becoming more fragmented as new models from legacy automakers arrive. Tesla's long-held dominance is facing credible challenges. Pricing remains a key lever, influenced by both manufacturer actions and incentive structures. Although specific inventory data is lacking for this period, the rising wholesale volume points to increased market churn. The newly implemented tariffs pose a particularly significant risk to the EV segment due to the complex global supply chains for batteries and other essential components, many of which originate from regions subject to tariffs, like China.

## V. Key Factors Driving Inventory Levels

Several interconnected factors converged to shape the vehicle inventory landscape in early April 2025, with the impact of newly announced tariffs being the most immediate and disruptive element.

### A. Impact of Tariffs

The announcement in late March of a 25% tariff on imported light vehicles (effective April 3, 2025) and a corresponding tariff on imported auto parts (scheduled for May 3, 2025) acted as the primary catalyst for the sharp inventory reduction observed at the start of April. Aware of the impending price increases, a significant number of consumers accelerated their purchase decisions, leading to a surge in sales during the final days of March. This pull-ahead effect rapidly depleted existing dealer stock, particularly for imported models or brands heavily reliant on imports.

This created significant near-term uncertainty. Analysts projected that the inventory imported before the tariff deadline would likely be exhausted by mid-May. Automaker responses varied in the immediate aftermath: Hyundai, Genesis, and Mercedes-Benz pledged to hold prices steady for a limited time; Volkswagen and Audi planned to add explicit import fees to vehicle pricing (with Audi initially halting US deliveries); Nissan announced price reductions on US-produced models like the Rogue and Pathfinder; and Ford introduced employee pricing on many models.

The anticipated consequences led major industry analysts, including Cox Automotive and S&P Global Mobility, to revise their full-year 2025 sales forecasts downward. S&P Global Mobility, for instance, downgraded its US sales forecast by 700,000 units for 2025 compared to its March projection. The complexity extends beyond fully assembled vehicles; the tariff on auto parts threatens to increase costs even for vehicles assembled domestically, given the global nature of automotive supply chains where components are sourced internationally. Electric vehicles, with their heavy reliance on internationally sourced battery materials and components, are seen as particularly vulnerable.

The tariffs represent a major external shock, disrupting market equilibrium. The pull-ahead phenomenon created an artificial inflation of demand in late March, masking underlying market conditions. The true impact will become clearer in the second and third quarters as higher costs associated with tariffs filter through to consumers via increased vehicle prices. This policy intervention introduces significant volatility, complicating inventory planning for dealers, production scheduling for manufacturers, and purchasing decisions for consumers. It also adds considerable risk to the market's recovery trajectory and may incentivize longer-term shifts in sourcing and production localization.

### B. Consumer Demand and Sales Pace

March 2025 witnessed an exceptionally strong sales performance, with volume estimates ranging from 1.56 to 1.59 million units. This significantly outperformed forecasts and resulted in the highest seasonally adjusted annual rate (SAAR) of sales, estimated at 17.8 million units, in four years. First-quarter sales overall showed year-over-year growth, and used vehicle sales were also notably robust in March.

However, this surge must be interpreted with caution. As discussed, it was heavily influenced by the tariff announcement pull-ahead effect. Underlying consumer demand faced headwinds even before this distortion. Consumer sentiment indices showed declines from January through March 2025, suggesting growing economic apprehension. While evidence of pent-up demand from previous years existed, the natural sales pace was expected to moderate in 2025. Dealertrack data showed unique credit applications remained strong year-over-year in early April but had trended down from the peak activity seen in the final week of March. Therefore, the March sales spike should not be viewed as indicative of sustainable underlying market strength but rather as a temporary distortion driven by external policy factors.

### C. Affordability Constraints

Vehicle affordability remained a significant and persistent challenge for consumers, acting as a fundamental constraint on the market.

For new vehicles, the average listing price hovered near \$48,000, and the average amount financed exceeded \$41,000 in Q1 2025. The average annual percentage rate (APR) for new vehicle loans was around 7.1%, although some easing in rates was noted in March and April. Despite this, affordability pressures were evident in financing trends: nearly one-fifth (19.8%) of new car buyers opted for 84-month loan terms in Q1, an all-time high. Furthermore, 17.7% of buyers committed to monthly payments exceeding \$1,000. While incentive spending had been generally increasing prior to March, the surge in demand led to a slight decrease in average incentive spending per unit in March, estimated at \$3,059 by J.D. Power. The tariff situation introduces uncertainty about future incentive levels.

In the used vehicle market, the average listing price was approximately \$25,180. While relatively flat year-over-year, this remains historically elevated. Compounding the price issue, average used car loan rates were exceptionally high, estimated around 14% APR. The scarcity of vehicles priced below \$15,000 (only 28 days' supply) particularly squeezed budget-conscious buyers.

These affordability pressures fundamentally limit the market's potential. High prices and financing costs strain household budgets, forcing consumers to extend loan terms to uncomfortably long durations, seek out older and scarcer used vehicles, or postpone purchases entirely. This acts as a natural brake on overall sales volume and makes the market particularly sensitive to further price increases, such as those anticipated from tariffs.

### D. Production and Supply Chain Factors

While the most acute supply chain disruptions of the pandemic era have subsided for new vehicles, the supply side of the market is not entirely free from constraints or risks. New vehicle inventory had shown significant recovery through 2024, approaching pre-pandemic levels by some metrics, although, as noted earlier, remaining measurably below 2019 levels according to other analyses.

The legacy of reduced production between 2020 and 2022 continues to cast a long shadow over the *used* vehicle market. The diminished output during those years directly translates into fewer 2-to-4-year-old vehicles, particularly off-lease units, entering the secondary market today. This structural deficit is a key reason why used vehicle inventory remains below pre-pandemic norms.

Looking forward, potential new challenges exist. Concerns about future semiconductor shortages, while speculative, were mentioned by some analysts. More concretely, the implementation of tariffs on imported auto parts introduces new risks to supply chain stability and cost structures for all manufacturers, regardless of final assembly location. The intricate global network for components, especially critical items like EV batteries often sourced from China, means tariffs can disrupt production flows and add costs throughout the value chain. Thus, while acute shortages are not the current primary issue for new cars, the supply side remains influenced by past disruptions (affecting used supply) and vulnerable to new geopolitical and policy-driven risks.

### E. Seasonality and Economic Indicators

Market activity in March and April is typically influenced by seasonal patterns. The arrival of spring often coincides with an uptick in sales activity, partly driven by consumers receiving tax refunds which can be used towards down payments. This seasonal tailwind likely contributed to the sales volume observed in March, alongside the tariff effect.

However, these seasonal factors operate within a broader economic context. As mentioned, key indicators like consumer sentiment showed weakness early in the year, and consumer spending growth reportedly slowed in March. Interest rates, while showing slight recent declines, remained elevated overall and a significant factor in purchase decisions. This suggests that while seasonality provided some support, underlying economic caution could temper demand moving forward, particularly once the artificial boost from the tariff pull-ahead dissipates.

## VI. Synthesis: Overall Vehicle Availability Estimate

Combining the inventory data for both new and used vehicles provides an estimate of the total number of vehicles available for sale on dealer lots across the United States at the beginning of April 2025.

Based primarily on Cox Automotive data reported in mid-April 2025 (reflecting inventory levels as of March 31st or the start of April), the estimated total available inventory was approximately **4.83 million units**.

- **New Vehicle Inventory:** 2.69 million units

- **Used Vehicle Inventory:** 2.14 million units

- **Combined Total:** **4.83 million units**

This estimate relies heavily on Cox Automotive's analysis of vAuto Live Market View data, capturing vehicles on both franchised and independent dealer lots (for used vehicles). It is crucial to recognize that this figure represents a snapshot taken during a period of significant market volatility triggered by the tariff announcement. Inventory levels, particularly for new vehicles, were actively decreasing at the time these measurements were likely finalized.

While a total inventory exceeding 4.8 million vehicles might seem substantial, the preceding analysis underscores the importance of context. The days' supply metrics for both new (70 days Cox, 43 days S&P) and used (39 days) vehicles were low, indicating tight supply relative to the recent sales pace. Furthermore, comparisons to historical norms reveal that inventory, especially for new vehicles and affordable used cars, remains constrained compared to pre-pandemic levels and faces structural limitations. Significant variations also exist across brands, models, and price segments. Therefore, the raw inventory number alone does not fully capture the availability challenges and market pressures present in early April 2025. The analysis of days' supply, historical trends, and segment breakdowns provides a more nuanced understanding of the actual market conditions and availability pressures faced by consumers and dealers.

## VII. Market Outlook and Strategic Implications

The U.S. automotive market stands at a critical juncture in the spring of 2025. The confluence of tariff implementations, persistent affordability challenges, and lingering supply chain effects creates a complex and uncertain environment.

**Near-Term Expectations (Next 1-3 Months):**

- **Inventory Constraints:** Both new and used vehicle inventories, particularly measured by days' supply, are expected to remain tight or potentially tighten further in the coming months. The depletion of pre-tariff new vehicle stock and the potential impact of parts tariffs on production and repair costs will likely constrain new supply. Used vehicle supply may also face pressure if consumers shift away from higher-priced new vehicles, exacerbating existing tightness, especially at lower price points.

- **Price Escalation:** Significant price increases for new vehicles appear highly probable as manufacturers and dealers pass on tariff-related costs. Used vehicle prices are also likely to face upward pressure, driven by spillover demand from the new market, ongoing tight supply, and already rising wholesale values observed in April.

- **Sales Moderation:** A slowdown in sales momentum is anticipated once the pull-ahead effect from March subsides and higher prices take hold. Downward revisions to full-year sales forecasts by multiple analysts reflect this expectation.

- **Market Volatility:** The market is expected to remain volatile, highly sensitive to further developments or clarifications regarding tariff policies and potential retaliatory actions.

**Strategic Implications for Stakeholders:**

- **Dealers:** Navigating this period requires exceptional agility in inventory management. Balancing the need to pass on increased acquisition costs with the imperative to maintain sales volume will be paramount. Sourcing desirable and affordable used inventory will remain a significant challenge but offers a crucial avenue to serve budget-conscious consumers. Transparent communication with customers regarding pricing and availability will be essential. Effectively utilizing market intelligence tools and optimizing operational efficiency can provide a competitive edge.

- **Manufacturers:** Face critical decisions regarding pricing strategies – determining how much of the tariff burden to absorb versus pass on to consumers, with potential consequences for market share. Production schedules must adapt to parts tariff impacts and potential shifts in consumer demand towards more affordable or domestically produced models. Incentive programs will require careful calibration to support sales without excessively eroding margins. The current environment may accelerate consideration of longer-term adjustments to supply chain sourcing and North American production footprints. Continued focus on developing cost-effective platforms, including for EVs, becomes increasingly important.

- **Consumers:** The purchasing environment is challenging. Those needing a vehicle in the immediate term might find dwindling opportunities to secure pre-tariff pricing. Affordability remains the primary obstacle. Prospective buyers should be prepared for potentially higher prices and possibly limited selection for certain models. Thorough research, exploring a wider range of brands and potentially considering certified pre-owned or other used options (while understanding the associated market tightness), and securing financing pre-approval are advisable strategies. Flexibility regarding specific features or models may be necessary.

- **Lenders:** Close monitoring of consumer affordability metrics, including loan-to-value ratios, payment-to-income levels, and loan term lengths, is crucial. While higher vehicle prices may lead to larger loan amounts, increased economic uncertainty and affordability strain could elevate credit risk and necessitate adjustments to underwriting standards. Default and repossession rates should be watched closely.

**Conclusion:**

The U.S. automotive inventory landscape in early April 2025 reflects a market significantly jolted by the imposition of import tariffs, layered upon pre-existing dynamics of affordability constraints and supply normalization challenges. The immediate outlook points towards continued inventory tightness, probable price increases across both new and used segments, and a likely moderation of the sales pace seen in late March. This volatile environment demands strategic foresight and adaptability from all market participants – manufacturers, dealers, lenders, and consumers alike. The longer-term trajectory of the market will hinge on the duration and ultimate impact of trade policies, the evolution of macroeconomic conditions influencing consumer confidence and purchasing power, and the industry's capacity to innovate and adjust its production, sourcing, and pricing models in response.
